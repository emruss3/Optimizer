Pretrained layout test: six of the 28 sites, drawn

Each band is one development site. Left is the plan the civil got approved, centre is the pretrained model's first try on the same boundary, right is the nearest clean training plan that the lookup control uses. The three panels in a band share one scale. Across the 28 sites the two tied: 0.53 pretrained, 0.57 lookup.

How the six were chosen

By rule from the scores, before anything was drawn. Multifamily and townhome: of the sites where the pretrained layout scored better than the lookup, the one with the median margin, and the same among the sites where it scored worse. Residential and mixed use: the site with the median margin. That gives typical cases, not the best or worst.

Read with care

All five residential sites scored better, so there is no worse residential case to show; four of the six bands are better cases against 17 of 28 overall. Mixed use has only two sites. The error compares counts, sizes, coverage, setback, parking and drive length, so a layout can score well and still look wrong.

What is drawn

Traced footprints for the civil and training plans, rectangles for the pretrained layout. Drives are centrelines drawn 24 ft wide. Traces include retained buildings. The lookup error uses the neighbour's measures scaled to this site's density.
